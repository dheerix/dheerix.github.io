Selected technical evidence

Four projects that show how I work when the system has consequences.

Not abstract success stories: source-backed examples of decisions, tensions, and production concerns that shaped my judgment.

01 / Openlane

Marketplace modernization

An automotive marketplace moving from legacy foundations toward cloud-native services and modern product surfaces—while production stayed live.

Situation & obstacle

Oracle- and Java-based systems were deeply connected to vehicle, bidding, sales, user, and dealer workflows. A one-time replacement was not viable.

My ownership

Backend, frontend, platform, and integration work: BFFs, customer experiences, shared patterns, production operations, and delivery coordination.

Architecture & decision

Integration layers and event-driven services became evolutionary bridges that allowed platform generations to coexist.

Result & lesson

The platform could move toward cloud-native services while preserving continuity. Modernization is sustained engineering, not a cutover.

02 / Openlane

Guardlane

A production-oriented AI moderation capability for safer and more reliable marketplace content processing.

Situation & obstacle

A working AI flow needed to become a capability that could be operated and trusted through ambiguity, model failure, and downstream failure.

My ownership

I owned the production-readiness path: workflow shape, stakeholder alignment, AI/backend/MLOps/DevOps concerns, planning, and communication.

Architecture & trade-off

Marketplace input flows through evaluation and policy decisions to fallback or human review, outcomes, and monitoring. The trade-off is automation against safe, observable handling of uncertainty.

Result & lesson

A production-readiness plan around reliability, governance, observability, and controlled rollout. A model response is not a production AI system.

03 / Openlane

AI Upload

An LLM-assisted workflow for dealer-provided content, treated as a product and platform workflow rather than a model demo.

Situation & obstacle

Dealer content work is repetitive and time-sensitive. AI assistance had to fit operations, provide review paths, and earn production trust.

My ownership

I helped shape delivery across AI, platform integration, backend and frontend concerns, production thinking, and cross-functional communication.

Architecture & trade-off

Dealer content enters an AI-assisted flow with operational validation before marketplace-ready output. The choice is assistance with human judgment—not false end-to-end automation.

Result & lesson

AI capability connected to a real operating workflow. Adoption depends on validation, observability, and shared workflow assumptions.

04 / Holland & Barrett

Enterprise search & product discovery

Commerce search, browsing, and promotions where relevance, business rules, product data, and runtime behavior meet.

Situation & obstacle

Customers needed to discover products through a changing catalog while supporting navigation, promotions, and production reliability.

My ownership

OneSearch, Elasticsearch, search APIs, promotion engine, ANTLR grammar enhancements, React features, backend APIs, and production investigation.

Architecture & decision

A React storefront was composed across search and browse paths, Elasticsearch, and commerce services for catalog, pricing, inventory, and promotions.

Result & lesson

Search is information retrieval and product discovery—not a database query. Business rules and relevance are architecture concerns.

Engineering principles

The principles I return to when a system becomes complicated.

  1. 01 · Evidence before optimization. Observe the request path, dependencies, and user impact before treating a symptom as a root cause.
  2. 02 · Reliability before cleverness. A simple design with known failure behavior is more valuable than an elegant flow that cannot be operated.
  3. 03 · Incremental modernization over rewrites. Let legacy and modern systems coexist through deliberate seams.
  4. 04 · Production readiness over demo readiness. Monitoring, fallback, deployment, and ownership are part of the feature.
  5. 05 · Own the system, not only the assigned code. The work continues through QA, rollout, incidents, feedback, and the next decision.
  6. 06 · Clear communication reduces delivery risk. Shared understanding of trade-offs and ownership keeps systems moving.
