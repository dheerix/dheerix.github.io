Engineering case study · Production AI

Guardlane

How a marketplace moderation capability evolved from one model call into an operable system with specialized detection paths and explicit policy boundaries.

Role
Software Engineer
Status
Production capability
Domains
Production AI · service architecture · MLOps · human review

Problem

Moderate short marketplace messages consistently without treating a probabilistic model as the owner of product policy.

My ownership

Owned delivery across requirements, backend orchestration, dataset and model development, SageMaker deployment, UI integration, human-review tooling, production readiness, and stakeholder alignment.

Outcome

Delivered a traceable moderation capability with a stable consumer contract, specialized inference paths, persisted outcomes, operational telemetry, and human review.

01 · Problem

Moderation was a systems problem, not only a classification problem.

Product pressure

Marketplace conversations could contain contact sharing, abusive content, negotiation attempts, and other policy violations. The workflow needed timely decisions while preserving the product context required to interpret a prediction correctly.

Technical pressure

Deterministic patterns, English semantic classification, and unsupported-language handling had different latency, cost, capability, and evaluation characteristics. Consumers still needed one predictable contract.

Operating pressure

A successful endpoint call could not prove prediction quality. The system also needed traceability, persisted evidence, operational visibility, and a way for reviewers to correct outcomes.

Ownership constraint

Guardlane could classify content, but the consuming marketplace workflow owned the final user-facing action. Keeping that boundary explicit allowed product policy to evolve without retraining the model.

03 · Key decisions

The architecture reflects explicit trade-offs.

These are compact summaries of the project ADRs. The emphasis is on why each boundary exists, not on reproducing internal documentation.

Hybrid moderation

Use the best-fit detector for each request class.

Why it mattered

No single technique met every capability, latency, cost, and evaluation need.

Chosen approach

Combine deterministic detection, an English multi-label DistilBERT classifier on SageMaker, and an LLM fallback for unsupported input.

Primary trade-off

Better specialization in exchange for orchestration complexity.

Policy boundary

Return prediction evidence; keep product action with the consumer.

Why it mattered

The same detected category can require different behavior in different workflow contexts.

Chosen approach

Guardlane normalizes evidence while the marketplace workflow applies contextual policy.

Primary trade-off

Cleaner ownership requires a carefully versioned consumer contract.

Unified contract

Hide engine-specific formats behind a stable response.

Why it mattered

Consumers should not understand model tensors, provider responses, or rule-engine internals.

Chosen approach

Normalize categories, sources, confidence evidence, versions, and processing status.

Primary trade-off

The orchestration layer must preserve useful detail without leaking implementations.

Asynchronous evidence

Separate inference latency from operational persistence.

Why it mattered

Review and traceability are valuable, but they should not unnecessarily extend the user-facing path.

Chosen approach

Publish moderation evidence for storage and later human review after producing a result.

Primary trade-off

Lower request coupling introduces delivery, retry, and evidence-completeness concerns.

05 · Business-impact investigation

Model quality is not product quality.

After rollout, the team observed an approximately 3% decrease in sale conversion. That correlation created a hypothesis that Guardlane or its decision policy could be contributing. It does not establish that Guardlane caused the movement.

Counterfactual

What would pass if negotiation alone were not blocking?

Current policy candidate

BLOCK = negotiation OR negative OR circumvention OR other violation

Alternative to evaluate

BLOCK = negative OR circumvention OR other violation
negotiation = context

The key cohort has negotiation present while contact, negative, circumvention, and other violations are absent. Its size, reviewed intent, exposure, and downstream behavior determine business plausibility—not the raw count of negotiation blocks.

Investigation questions
  1. What share of blocked messages contains negotiation?
  2. What share is blocked only because of negotiation?
  3. How much negotiation appears commercially legitimate?
  4. Which labels commonly co-occur with negotiation?
  5. Is conversion movement concentrated among exposed users?
  6. Is the affected population large enough to explain the movement?
  7. What undesirable interactions would become allowed?
  8. Can negotiation become contextual rather than blocking?
  9. Which experiment, guardrails, and rollback criteria validate a change?

Correlation ≠ causation. Stronger evidence requires exposure analysis, counterfactual replay, reviewed intent, a controlled and reversible policy change, business and safety guardrails, and checks for concurrent marketplace changes.

07 · Learning

What changed in my architectural judgment.

What the project taught me

The production system is the orchestration, contract, policy boundary, telemetry, review path, and recovery behavior—not simply the model endpoint. Evaluation must match each component’s actual capability.

What I would strengthen

I would make partial-processing states and policy versions explicit from the first release, validate latency budgets against tail behavior, and design representative review sampling alongside targeted review.

What would trigger re-evaluation

New language coverage, material taxonomy change, unacceptable review disagreement, dependency cost or latency shifts, and repeated policy-adapter divergence would justify revisiting the routing or ownership model.

Outcome statement

Guardlane established a reusable moderation capability and an operational learning loop. A roughly 3% conversion decrease was observed after rollout; no causal contribution or post-change result is claimed while the investigation continues.
