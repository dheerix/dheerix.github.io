## Openlane

**Senior Software Engineer**

My work here has centered on modernizing legacy marketplace systems and delivering new capabilities across platform, frontend, backend, and AI-enabled workflows. I’ve helped evolve Oracle- and Java-based systems toward cloud-native, event-driven services while preserving production continuity and supporting incremental migration.

Key areas of contribution:

- Modernizing the Vehicle Detail experience and backend-for-frontend layer so multiple internal services could be aggregated into a cleaner customer-facing API.
- Building and maintaining product experiences with React, Svelte, and Stencil while contributing to shared UI patterns that improved consistency across marketplace surfaces.
- Supporting AI-enabled workflows such as Upload Anything, where production AI, human review, and marketplace operations all had to work together reliably.
- Migrating services from AWS SDK for JavaScript v2 to v3, improving packaging, runtime behavior, testing confidence, and deployment reliability.
- Supporting production operations, technical onboarding, architecture discussions, and cross-functional delivery across product and platform initiatives.

What I’m proud of most is the combination of breadth and ownership: being able to work across modern frontend code, backend services, integration layers, and production support while helping the organization move forward without destabilizing what already worked.

Technologies: .NET, Node.js, React, TypeScript, Svelte, Stencil, AWS, Lambda, Kubernetes, SageMaker, Oracle, Docker, Honeycomb

This was a strong example of technical work that had direct business consequences. Search was not just a feature; it was a core part of how customers found products and how the business shaped conversion.

Technologies: Elasticsearch, React, .NET, backend APIs, ANTLR, commerce search, promotions

## Earlier Career

Before the later platform, AI, and leadership work, I built software across Java, C#, .NET, Android, PHP, and SQL environments in desktop, mobile, and enterprise settings.

That earlier period gave me the foundation I still rely on today: understanding how to build real software, move across technologies, think in systems, and adapt as the problem changes.

It is also where the pattern emerged that has defined the rest of my career:

- start with the problem, not the framework

- build for maintainability, not just completion
- keep expanding the kinds of problems I can solve
