# Project 1 — Guardlane

## Domain

Artificial Intelligence Platform Engineering

---

## Business Context

Organizations adopting Large Language Models require governance mechanisms to ensure AI-generated content is safe, compliant, observable, and aligned with business policies.

Guardlane was built to provide a production-ready moderation and governance platform that combines automated AI evaluation with human review workflows.

---

## Engineering Challenges

- Production AI reliability
- Human-in-the-loop moderation
- AI governance
- Model integration
- Evaluation metrics
- Operational visibility
- Scalable moderation workflows

---

## My Contributions

- AI moderation workflows
- Backend service development
- Platform architecture
- Dashboard implementation
- Production deployments
- Cross-functional collaboration
- Technical planning

---

## Technologies

- .NET
- AWS

- TypeScript
- React
- AI/LLM integrations
- Cloud-native services

## Engineering Lessons

This project taught me that successful AI systems depend as much on governance, monitoring, and operational processes as they do on the underlying models.

Building trustworthy AI requires disciplined engineering practices beyond model integration.

# Common Themes

Although these projects belong to different industries, they share several engineering principles.

## Platform Thinking

Rather than solving isolated problems, each project involved building reusable capabilities that support multiple business workflows.

---

## Modernization

Each project required balancing innovation with operational stability.

---

## Production Engineering

Every project reinforced the importance of monitoring, deployment practices, observability, and long-term maintainability.

---

## Cross-Functional Collaboration

Successful delivery depended on effective communication with product managers, designers, QA engineers, platform teams, and fellow software engineers.

---

## Continuous Learning

Each project introduced unfamiliar technologies and architectural patterns, reinforcing the value of transferable engineering principles over framework-specific knowledge.
